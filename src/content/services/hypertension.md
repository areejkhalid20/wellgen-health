  - q: "What happens to the body when blood pressure rises?"
    a: "When blood pressure rises and consistently exceeds 130/80 mmHg, the arteries—which are designed to be flexible and resilient—gradually become stiffer and narrower due to the constant pressure. This reduces their ability to deliver oxygen-rich blood efficiently, forcing the heart to exert more effort. Over time, this excessive workload can lead to left ventricular hypertrophy (thickening of the heart muscle), increased risk of arterial plaque buildup, and eventual organ strain, particularly in the kidneys where delicate filtration structures are sensitive to pressure changes."
  - q: "How is blood pressure measured, and what are normal versus high readings?"
    a: "Blood pressure measures the force exerted by circulating blood on the walls of your arteries and is recorded using two key numbers: Systolic Pressure (the top number), which indicates the pressure in your arteries when your heart contracts and pumps blood, and Diastolic Pressure (the bottom number), which reflects the pressure when your heart relaxes between beats. Normal blood pressure typically ranges around 120/80 mmHg. Consistent readings above 130/80 mmHg are considered high and cause arteries to stiffen, straining the cardiovascular system."

This interdependence means patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) often have high blood pressure, and those with uncontrolled hypertension are at elevated risk for kidney decline. ## Understanding hypertension

Hypertension (high blood pressure) is a silent, chronic condition where persistently high force against artery walls strains the heart, vessels, brain, and kidneys. This sustained pressure is the primary driver of cardiovascular damage. Unmanaged, it drastically increases the risk of stroke and renal failure.

### The silent danger & causes

Blood pressure measures force when the heart pumps (Systolic) and rests (Diastolic). Consistent readings above 130/80 mmHg cause arteries to stiffen.

- **Hypertension–Kidney Axis:** High pressure damages kidney vessels; conversely, kidney issues raise pressure, a dangerous cycle.
- **Types:** Most cases are Primary (genetics/lifestyle); Secondary stems from causes like kidney disease or sleep apnea.
