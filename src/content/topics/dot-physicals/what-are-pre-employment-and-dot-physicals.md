## The purpose of pre-employment and DOT physicals

Both pre-employment physicals and DOT physicals serve a shared purpose; to ensure that workers are medically fit to safely perform their job functions. These exams protect the health and safety of the individual, their coworkers, and the public by identifying potential medical issues that could impact job performance, cause injury, or interfere with critical job responsibilities.

While pre-employment physicals are tailored to meet specific job requirements set by an employer or organization, DOT physicals are federally mandated for individuals operating commercial motor vehicles (CMVs) under the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA).

## Pre-employment physicals: ensuring workforce readiness and safety

A pre-employment physical is a vital part of the hiring process for many employers across the United States. It's designed to evaluate whether a candidate has the physical capability and overall health to perform essential job duties without risking their own well-being or that of others. These evaluations are especially important in physically demanding or safety-sensitive industries such as transportation, warehousing, manufacturing, emergency response, and construction, where even minor health issues could pose serious risks.

## Why employers require pre-employment physicals

Employers request these physicals for several key reasons:

- **Workplace Safety:** Identifying potential health concerns before employment reduces the risk of injuries and on-the-job accidents.
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Certain industries must meet Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) or Department of Labor standards that require medical clearance for specific tasks.
- **Job Performance:** Ensuring that employees are physically capable of meeting job demands enhances productivity and minimizes long-term absences due to health-related issues.
- **Health Baseline Establishment:** Employers gain a clear picture of a new hire's baseline health, allowing for better future workplace wellness planning.

## What to expect during a DOT physical?

A DOT physical at WellGen Health is designed to be thorough yet efficient, ensuring all FMCSA standards are met. During your exam, our clinicians will:

- Review your complete medical history, including medications, past illnesses, and chronic conditions.
- Conduct a vision test to ensure you meet FMCSA visual acuity standards (at least 20/40 in each eye, with or without corrective lenses).
- Perform a hearing test to verify your ability to detect normal conversation or a forced whisper at the required distance.
- Check blood pressure and heart rate to assess cardiovascular health and endurance.
- Complete a urinalysis to screen for diabetes, kidney issues, or other underlying conditions.
- Evaluate neurological and musculoskeletal function to ensure you can operate commercial vehicles safely.

If you meet all health requirements, you'll receive a Medical Examiner's Certificate (MEC), valid for up to two years. In some cases, individuals with controlled medical conditions (such as hypertension or diabetes) may require more frequent re-evaluation to maintain certification.
