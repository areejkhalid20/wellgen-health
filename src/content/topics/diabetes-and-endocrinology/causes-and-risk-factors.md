## Family history and genetics

Having a parent, sibling or close relative with diabetes significantly raises your risk. In type 2 diabetes, inherited traits affect how your body makes and uses insulin. In type 1, certain genes make the immune system more likely to attack the cells that produce insulin.

If diabetes runs in your family, we recommend early screening so we can assess your risk and start prevention sooner.

## Weight and where it's carried

Extra weight, especially around the waist, is one of the most common risk factors you can change. Fat around the organs makes the body respond less well to insulin (insulin resistance). The pancreas then has to work harder, and over time blood sugar rises.

Our endocrinologists and nutrition team build weight plans around eating, activity and regular checks. The goal is metabolic health, not appearance.

## Low activity

Long hours of sitting and little exercise make your muscles less able to use sugar for energy, which raises blood sugar. Regular movement improves insulin sensitivity, helps with weight and protects your heart. We create activity plans that fit your health and fitness level.

## Diet

Meals high in refined carbohydrates, sugary drinks, processed foods and saturated fats cause blood sugar spikes and weight gain. Our dietitians help you build meals around whole grains, fiber, lean protein and healthy fats, and show you how different foods affect your blood sugar.

## Hormone conditions

Diabetes is closely linked to the hormone system. Some conditions raise the risk:

- **PCOS:** often causes insulin resistance.
- **Cushing's syndrome:** too much cortisol raises blood sugar.
- **Thyroid disorders:** both an underactive and an overactive thyroid can affect how your body handles sugar.

Our endocrinology team screens for and treats these conditions early.

## Stress and sleep

Long-term stress keeps stress hormones high, which raises blood sugar and disrupts insulin. Poor or irregular sleep changes the hormones that control hunger, leading to more appetite and weight gain. We include stress support, relaxation techniques and sleep advice in your care.

## Age, ethnicity and pregnancy history

- Type 2 risk rises after age 40, though it is becoming more common in younger adults.
- People of South Asian, African, Hispanic and Native American descent have a higher risk.
- Women who had gestational diabetes, or who have PCOS, are at higher risk.

We tailor prevention to your personal risk, including food advice that fits your culture and routine.

## Environment and medicines

Some chemicals, pollutants and medicines can interfere with hormones and insulin. Busy, high-stress lifestyles and long screen time also play a part.

## Metabolic flexibility

This is your body's ability to switch between burning sugar and burning fat for energy. Overeating, inactivity and stress can reduce it, leading to high blood sugar and weight gain. Balanced eating, regular movement and regular check-ups help restore it.

## What you can do

You can't change your genes or your age, but most other risk factors can be improved. Early screening and a prevention plan made with your provider are the best place to start.
