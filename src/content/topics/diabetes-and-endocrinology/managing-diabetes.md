## Treatment based on your data

Your plan is built around your own numbers. Using continuous glucose monitoring and your test results, your provider fine-tunes:

- **Insulin:** the type, dose, timing and how it's given (injection or pump).
- **Non-insulin injections:** such as GLP-1 medicines, which help with blood sugar and weight.
- **Tablets:** such as metformin, DPP-4 inhibitors and SGLT2 inhibitors for type 2 diabetes.

Every change is based on how your body responds, to improve energy, support weight and reduce side effects. Telehealth visits and remote monitoring keep you in touch with your team between appointments.

## Habits that last

Medication is only part of the picture.

- **Medical nutrition therapy:** a dietitian builds an eating plan that suits your needs, tastes and culture. You learn to balance carbohydrates, protein and fat, read food labels and time meals around your medication.
- **Exercise:** walking, yoga, strength training or gym workouts, chosen to improve insulin sensitivity, heart health and mood.
- **Health coaching:** practical help with weight, sleep and stress, focused on changes you can keep.

## Preventing complications

High blood sugar over many years can lead to heart disease and stroke, nerve damage, kidney disease, eye damage and foot ulcers. To catch problems early, we regularly check your:

- Blood pressure and cholesterol
- Kidney function
- Nerve sensation in your feet
- Eyes and feet, with routine exams

## Care for related hormone conditions

Diabetes often comes with other hormone conditions, and we treat them together:

- **Thyroid disorders:** including Hashimoto's disease, Graves' disease and thyroid nodules.
- **Adrenal and pituitary disorders:** including Cushing's syndrome, Addison's disease and growth hormone imbalances.
- **Reproductive hormones:** PCOS, irregular periods and fertility concerns linked to insulin resistance.
- **Bone health:** diabetes raises the risk of osteoporosis and vitamin D deficiency, so we check bone density when needed.

## Learning and support

You should understand both what to do and why. Through one-to-one sessions and workshops you learn to check your blood sugar, spot highs and lows, use insulin and a CGM, and read your glucose trends. Peer support and mental health care help with the emotional side of living with diabetes.

## Tools that keep you connected

Continuous glucose monitors, smart glucose meters, telehealth visits and apps for tracking meals and progress help you and your care team stay on top of your health between visits.
