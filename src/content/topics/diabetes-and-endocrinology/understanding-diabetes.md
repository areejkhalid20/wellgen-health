## How diabetes works

When you eat, carbohydrates turn into glucose (sugar) in your blood. Insulin, a hormone made by the pancreas, moves that glucose into your cells to give you energy. With diabetes, your body either doesn't make enough insulin or can't use it properly, so sugar builds up in your blood.

Over time, high blood sugar can damage your heart, kidneys, nerves and eyes. That is why diabetes is treated as a long-term condition to manage, not a single illness to cure.

## Types of diabetes

### Type 1 diabetes

Usually found in childhood or early adulthood. The immune system attacks the cells in the pancreas that make insulin, so people with type 1 need insulin for life. Our endocrinologists help with glucose monitoring, insulin adjustments, nutrition and activity.

### Type 2 diabetes

The most common type. The body resists insulin, or the pancreas can't keep up. Diet, low activity, excess weight and family history all play a part. It often develops quietly over years, so early testing makes a big difference. We offer lifestyle programs, nutrition counseling and medication management.

### Gestational diabetes

Develops during pregnancy, when hormone changes make the body less sensitive to insulin. It usually clears after birth but raises the chance of type 2 later. We help keep blood sugar safe for both mother and baby.

Less common forms of diabetes can be caused by genetic conditions, some medicines or diseases of the pancreas.

## Signs and symptoms

Symptoms can be mild at first. See a provider if you notice:

- Feeling very thirsty and passing urine often
- Feeling hungry all the time, or losing weight without trying
- Blurred vision
- Tiredness or weakness
- Cuts that heal slowly, or frequent infections
- Numbness or tingling in your hands or feet

We offer fasting glucose tests, A1C tests and glucose tolerance tests to check.

## Why early detection matters

Prediabetes means your blood sugar is higher than normal but not yet in the diabetes range. Finding it early gives you the chance to prevent or delay type 2 diabetes with changes to eating, activity, stress and regular blood sugar checks.

## Complications we help you prevent

Diabetes that isn't well controlled can lead to:

- Heart attack, stroke and high blood pressure
- Kidney damage (diabetic nephropathy)
- Nerve damage (diabetic neuropathy)
- Eye damage that can affect your sight (diabetic retinopathy)
- Foot ulcers and infections

Regular monitoring and early treatment greatly reduce these risks.

## Care from one connected team

Your care is led by endocrinologists working with dietitians, diabetes educators and wellness coaches. Depending on your needs, it can include continuous glucose monitoring, insulin pump therapy, meal planning, exercise guidance and emotional support, so you feel confident making everyday decisions about your health.
