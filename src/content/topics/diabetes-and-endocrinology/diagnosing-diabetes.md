## Why early diagnosis matters

Finding diabetes early can:

- Bring blood sugar under control before organs are damaged
- Prevent or delay heart disease, kidney damage and nerve damage
- Improve your energy, mood and quality of life
- Help you make informed choices about food and lifestyle

## Blood sugar tests

These are the standard tests for diabetes:

| Test | How it works | Points to diabetes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fasting blood glucose | Measured after not eating overnight | 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate tests |
| A1C | Your average blood sugar over the past 2 to 3 months | 6.5% or higher |
| Oral glucose tolerance test | Measures how your body handles a sugary drink | Used for gestational diabetes and prediabetes |
| Random blood glucose | Taken at any time when you have symptoms | 200 mg/dL or higher |

## Continuous glucose monitoring

A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) is a small sensor that tracks your blood sugar day and night. It shows how food, exercise, stress and sleep affect your levels, and lets your provider:

- Spot changes that one-off tests can miss
- See spikes after meals and lows during the night
- Adjust your medication and eating plan using real data

## Hormone tests

Because diabetes is closely tied to your hormones, we may test more than blood sugar:

- **Thyroid:** an underactive or overactive thyroid affects how your body uses sugar.
- **Adrenal glands:** cortisol affects how well insulin works.
- **Pituitary gland:** growth hormone and other imbalances can affect blood sugar.
- **Insulin and C-peptide:** show how much insulin you make, which helps identify the type of diabetes.

## Scans when needed

If a gland problem is suspected, your provider may recommend an ultrasound of the thyroid, pancreas or adrenal glands, or an MRI or CT scan of the pituitary or adrenal glands. These can find conditions such as Cushing's syndrome, thyroid disorders or pituitary growths that affect blood sugar.

## Understanding your results

Your endocrinologist looks at your blood tests, any scans and your medical history together. You get a clear explanation of:

- How your pancreas, liver, thyroid and adrenal glands work together
- How insulin resistance develops
- What your results mean for daily life
- What the next steps are

## From diagnosis to a plan

Your results become the starting point for a plan made with you. It may include changes to diet and lifestyle, medication or insulin, hormone treatment, stress support and regular follow-up tests. Diagnosis isn't a one-time event: regular check-ups keep your plan on track.
